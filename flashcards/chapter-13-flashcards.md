## Chapter 13: Full DY0-001 Exam Simulation and Time-Management Strategy — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | DY0-001 target pace per question? | ~1.8 minutes per question across up to 90 questions. Exam tip: falling significantly behind this pace by question 30 signals you need to start flagging more aggressively instead of fully solving hard items. |
| 2 | If 45 minutes remain for 12 unfinished PBQs, what's the available time per PBQ? | ~3.75 minutes per PBQ. Exam tip: use this quick division to decide whether to fully attempt each PBQ or triage by confidence level. |
| 3 | Should you ever leave a question blank if there's no guessing penalty? | No — always submit an answer. A blank guarantees zero credit; a guess has nonzero expected value. Exam tip: fill in every question before time runs out, even randomly. |
| 4 | What does 'flag for review' actually do? | Marks a question for revisit later without affecting scoring — it lets you commit a provisional answer and move on to maintain pace. Exam tip: it is a navigation tool only, not a scoring mechanism. |
| 5 | PBQ triage strategy vs. multiple-choice pacing — what's the key difference? | Multiple-choice: answer at steady ~1.8 min pace, flag uncertain ones. PBQs: attempt highest-confidence ones first to bank guaranteed points before tackling harder ones. Exam tip: don't apply the same time budget to both question types. |
| 6 | Domain-weighted review priority order? | Review flagged questions from the heaviest-weighted domain first, then descend to lighter domains. Exam tip: fixing a mistake in a 30%-weighted domain has more score impact than one in a 10%-weighted domain. |
| 7 | Elimination technique for expert-level distractors? | Watch for answer choices that add conditions or scope not stated in the scenario (e.g., extra services, extra steps). Eliminate the option that exceeds the stated requirement. Exam tip: 'more thorough-sounding' is not the same as 'correct.' |
| 8 | Mental math approach for statistics questions under time pressure? | Use order-of-magnitude estimation to eliminate implausible answer choices rather than computing exact values. Exam tip: sufficient when answer choices are spread far apart numerically. |
| 9 | Final-pass checklist — 3 key items? | 1) Confirm no question is left unanswered. 2) Re-check flagged items in domain-weight priority order. 3) Verify PBQ inputs/configs were actually saved. Exam tip: skipping the PBQ save-check is a common cause of lost points. |
| 10 | Should you change an answer based on gut feeling alone with no new information? | No — only change an answer if you find a concrete reasoning error or new insight. Exam tip: second-guessing without justification tends to reduce scores more often than it helps. |
| 11 | When should you start flagging more aggressively instead of fully solving each question? | As soon as you notice you're falling behind the ~1.8 min/question pace (e.g., significantly behind by question 30 of 90). Exam tip: pace-checking periodically prevents a last-minute time crisis. |
| 12 | Recommended split of remaining time between multiple-choice review and PBQ completion in the final stretch? | Prioritize completing/attempting all reachable PBQs (higher effort-to-point ratio and risk of zero credit if unattempted) before spending leftover time re-checking multiple-choice answers. Exam tip: multiple-choice re-checks are lower priority once initial answers are locked in. |
