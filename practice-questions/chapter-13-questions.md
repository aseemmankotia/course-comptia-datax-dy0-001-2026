**Q1.** The DY0-001 exam allows up to 90 questions in a total time budget built around roughly 1.8 minutes per question, and includes several performance-based questions (PBQs) mixed with multiple-choice items. Which time-management approach BEST maximizes your score?

A) Answer questions strictly in the order presented, spending equal time on every question including PBQs
B) Move quickly through multiple-choice questions at the target pace, flag PBQs and any uncertain items, then return to flagged questions with remaining time
C) Skip all PBQs entirely and only submit answers for multiple-choice questions to save time
D) Spend the first half of the exam exclusively on PBQs since they are worth more, then rush multiple-choice questions

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

Multiple-choice questions are answered faster and more reliably at a steady pace; flagging PBQs preserves time for the slower, higher-effort items and lets you use whatever time remains without jeopardizing the faster questions.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Equal time per question ignores that PBQs take disproportionately longer, causing you to either rush PBQs or run out of time for later multiple-choice items
- Skipping PBQs entirely forfeits guaranteed partial or full credit that could be earned even under time pressure
- Front-loading PBQs risks leaving too little time for the larger volume of multiple-choice questions, which are faster points to secure

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

**Q2.** A statistics-based question on the exam gives you a small dataset and asks which value is closest to the standard deviation, under a 1.8-minute time budget. Which approach is MOST appropriate?

A) Calculate the exact standard deviation using the full formula, including variance and square root, before looking at the answer choices
B) Guess randomly since standard deviation calculations cannot be done without a calculator
C) Skip the question immediately without attempting any estimation
D) Estimate the spread of the data using a rough order-of-magnitude check, then eliminate answer choices that are clearly too large or too small

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

A quick range/magnitude estimate lets you eliminate implausible answer choices fast, which is sufficient when the answers are spaced far apart — this fits the time budget and still produces a confident answer.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- A full exact calculation is too slow for a 1.8-minute budget and is rarely necessary when answer choices are far apart
- Random guessing discards the value of quick estimation, which usually narrows the choices to one or two candidates
- Skipping without any estimation wastes a question that could likely be answered correctly with minimal effort

</details>

**Q3.** Before starting your final review pass, you note the exam's domains are weighted 30%, 25%, 20%, 15%, and 10%. You have 20 minutes left and several flagged questions across all domains. In what order should you review flagged questions?

A) Review flagged questions in the exact order they appear on screen, regardless of domain
B) Review flagged questions from the domain weighted at 10% first, since it has the fewest questions to check
C) Review only questions you are completely unsure about, ignoring domain weight entirely
D) Review flagged questions from the heaviest-weighted domains first, working down to the lightest

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

With limited time, correcting mistakes in the heaviest-weighted domains has the largest impact on your overall score, so those flagged items should be reviewed first.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- On-screen order ignores impact — a low-weight domain question at the top of the list could consume time better spent on a high-weight domain question
- Prioritizing the lightest-weighted domain first wastes limited review time on questions with the smallest scoring impact
- Ignoring domain weight entirely means you might spend your remaining minutes on low-impact questions instead of high-impact ones

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q4.** A scenario question presents two answer choices that both technically satisfy the requirement described, but one choice adds an extra condition ('and configure geo-replication') that was never mentioned in the scenario. Using expert-level elimination technique, what should you do?

A) Choose the answer with the extra condition because more thorough solutions are always safer on certification exams
B) Eliminate both answers since neither can be correct if they overlap in functionality
C) Choose randomly between the two since they appear equally valid
D) Eliminate the answer with the extra, unstated condition and choose the option that matches the scenario's stated scope exactly

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

Expert-level distractors are often correct-looking options that introduce scope beyond what the scenario asked for; the best answer matches the stated requirements exactly, without unnecessary additions.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Adding unstated requirements is a classic distractor pattern designed to catch over-cautious test-takers — it is not inherently 'safer'
- Overlapping functionality between two choices doesn't disqualify both; one is usually the precise match and the other is the over-engineered distractor
- Guessing between two options ignores an available and reliable elimination signal (scope mismatch)

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q5.** With 5 minutes remaining and 3 unanswered multiple-choice questions, and assuming no penalty for incorrect guesses on this exam, what is the BEST action?

A) Select an answer for every remaining question before time expires, even if it's a guess
B) Leave the questions blank since guessing wrong looks worse than not answering
C) Spend the full 5 minutes trying to fully reason through only one of the three questions
D) Exit the exam early to avoid the stress of running out of time

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: A**

Since there is no penalty for wrong answers, a submitted guess has a chance of being correct while a blank answer guarantees zero credit — always fill in every question before time runs out.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Leaving answers blank guarantees no credit, which is strictly worse than guessing when there's no penalty
- Spending all remaining time on one question forfeits any chance of credit on the other two unanswered questions
- Exiting early forfeits all remaining unanswered questions and any chance to review flagged items

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q6.** What is the PRIMARY purpose of using the 'flag for review' function during the exam rather than immediately committing to an answer on a difficult question?

A) It automatically awards partial credit for flagged questions
B) It removes the question from your final score calculation
C) It shows you the correct answer once you flag three questions in a row
D) It lets you record a best-guess answer now and revisit the question later without losing time on other questions in the meantime

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

Flagging preserves your pace by letting you submit a provisional answer and move on, while still giving you the option to revisit the question during a final review pass if time allows.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Flagging has no effect on scoring or partial credit — it is purely a navigation and review tool
- Flagged questions are still scored normally; they are not excluded
- No certification exam interface reveals correct answers based on flag count — this is not a real feature

</details>

**Q7.** A candidate answers 60 multiple-choice questions in 90 minutes (steady pace) and has 12 PBQs remaining with 45 minutes left. Each PBQ is estimated to take 3-4 minutes to complete thoroughly. What is the BEST final-stretch strategy?

A) Attempt to fully complete all 12 PBQs in order, accepting that some may be left unfinished if time runs out
B) Quickly triage the 12 PBQs, attempting the ones you're most confident about first to bank guaranteed points, then use remaining time on harder PBQs
C) Skip PBQs entirely and use the remaining 45 minutes solely to re-check multiple-choice answers
D) Spend all 45 minutes on the single PBQ that appears first, ignoring the other 11

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

With roughly 3.75 minutes per PBQ available (45 minutes / 12), triaging by confidence secures the achievable points first, reducing the risk of leaving easy PBQs incomplete due to time running out on harder ones.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Working strictly in order risks running out of time on easy, high-confidence PBQs near the end while stuck on a hard one earlier
- Ignoring all 12 PBQs forfeits significant available points when time still exists to attempt them
- Fixating on a single PBQ wastes time that could secure partial credit across multiple questions

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q8.** Which item belongs on a proper final-pass review checklist before submitting the exam?

B) Re-read every single question from scratch regardless of remaining time
C) Submit immediately once the last question is reached, without reviewing flags
D) Confirm every question shows an answer (none left blank), re-check flagged items in domain-weight priority order, and verify PBQ inputs were saved

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

A disciplined final pass verifies no question is left unanswered, addresses flagged items by domain impact, and confirms PBQ work was actually saved — these are concrete, high-value checks within limited time.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Changing answers based on gut feeling alone, without new information or reasoning, statistically tends to lower scores more often than it helps
- Re-reading every question from scratch is rarely feasible in the time remaining and ignores prioritization
- Submitting without any review wastes the opportunity to catch blank answers or unsaved PBQ work

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>
